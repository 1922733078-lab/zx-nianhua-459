(a) Impressionism: small, visible brushstrokes; open composition; emphasis on the accurate
    depiction of changing natural light; ordinary modern-life subjects; outdoor scenes.
(b) Post-Impressionism: extends Impressionism toward bolder non-naturalistic color, symbolic
    or emotional content, distorted form and thick, distinctive brushwork.
(c) Expressionism: radically subjective perspective; agitated brushwork; intense, often
    non-naturalistic color; emotional anguish, angst or raw inner states.
(d) Cubism: objects broken into geometric fragments and reassembled; multiple viewpoints
    shown simultaneously; flattened, analytic or collage-like structure.
(e) Realism: truthful, unidealized depiction of ordinary everyday life and common people;
    restrained naturalistic detail; no dramatization.
(f) Baroque: strong chiaroscuro (dramatic light-shadow contrast); dynamic movement and
    grandeur; rich deep color; religious or mythological drama.
(g) Symbolism: rejection of direct representation; dreamlike, mystical or allegorical imagery;
    suggestive rather than explicit content; decorative line; spiritual and literary themes.
(h) Romanticism: emphasis on emotion and the sublime; dramatic skies, ruins and wild
    nature; awe, individualism, exotic or heroic themes.
(i) Art Nouveau: sinuous "whiplash" organic lines; flat decorative floral patterns; ornamental
    integration of figure and design; poster-like elegance.
(j) Northern Renaissance: 15th-16th-century Netherlandish and German precision; meticulous
    oil technique; hidden symbolism in domestic objects; crisp detail and luminous glazes.
