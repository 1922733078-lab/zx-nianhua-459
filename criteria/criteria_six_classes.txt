(a) Martial door god: armored military figure(s) with banner-flags, painted or full-beard face,
wielding mace, whip, spear, saber, halberd or axe; single standing or mounted, or symmetric
confronting pairs (Qin Qiong / Yuchi Gong type).
(b) Infant/child motif: round-faced child(ren) with hair buns as the main subject, holding fish,
lotus, lantern or antiques; "surplus year after year" and "hundred sons" themes; armed children
still count.
(c) Lady/beauty: adult woman as the main subject, holding fan, flowers, willow branch or ruyi;
beauty portraits and spring-outing scenes.
(d) Civil-official deity: figure wearing official hat or crown and court robe, holding hu-tablet,
ruyi or gold ingot, seated or standing — wealth god, Tianguan, kitchen god, Fu-Lu-Shou, Guanyin,
Wenchang, Zhong Kui, and deities before altars.
(e) Opera scene: three or more figures in narrative interaction, generals in battle, chariot
fights and siege scenes, opera stages with title captions.
(f) Auspicious non-figural: no human subject — guardian tiger, lion, rooster, zodiac animals,
gourd and antique still-life, meridian figure charts, printing blocks and couplet fragments.
